The GAIN Pitch Playbook

A comprehensive, step-by-step guide to delivering a compelling business case for the Global Armenian Impact Network.

Phase 1: Pre-Pitch Preparation

The 90% of the Work

Success is determined before you walk in the room. This is your checklist for building an unshakeable foundation.

1. Know Your Audience

Map your board members: identify the champion, the skeptic, and the financial expert. Tailor points to their specific interests.

2. Define Your "One-Sentence Ask"

Be crystal clear about what you want. Example: "We request approval for a 6-month MVP phase with a budget of [X]."

3. Build Your "Proof Arsenal"

Have your data ready (surveys, benchmarks) to deploy as evidence when questions arise.

4. Rehearse & Pressure Test

Conduct a dry run with a friendly audience, then a "Red Team" session with tough questions. Time your pitch to 15-20 mins.

Phase 2: The Pitch Narrative (Delivery)

Step 1: The Hook (The "What If...")

Pitcher's Action

Start with the inspiring vision, not the agenda. Capture their imagination by painting a picture of a connected, empowered future. Sell the destination before showing the map.

Startup Framing

This is the **Vision Sale**. You are selling the massive potential and the inspiring future state. Great startups sell a compelling vision first.

Step 2: The Problem (The "Why Now?")

Pitcher's Action

Frame fragmentation as untapped potential and establish urgency. The cost of inaction is leaving our greatest asset—collective intelligence—on the table.

Startup Framing

Define the **Market Opportunity**. Show the scale with the **Total Addressable Market** (Global Armenian Nation) and your focus with the **Serviceable Addressable Market** (key professional/student groups).

Step 3: The Solution (The "How")

Pitcher's Action

Position GAIN as the elegant "network of networks"—an integrator, not a competitor. Introduce the platform as the powerful engine for this ecosystem.

Critical Questions to Address
  • Is this truly a unique idea? (Answer: Yes, we are an integrator).
  • Why is AIFA the right organization to lead this? (Answer: Credibility, reach, long-term mission).
Startup Framing

Define the **Product**. GAIN is a **multi-sided platform** whose value increases for everyone as more users join, creating powerful network effects.

Step 4: The Value (The "What's In It For Them?")

Pitcher's Action

Speak to the "Why," not just the "What." Connect every feature to a core human need for connection, growth, or contribution. Show tangible value for each user type.

Startup Framing

Explain the **Business Model**. It's based on creating a virtuous cycle of value through **Network Effects**, making the platform more powerful and self-sustaining over time.

Step 5: The Strategy (The "How We'll Win")

Pitcher's Action

Build confidence by presenting a smart, phased, data-driven plan. Explain the "Tipping Point" strategy to show you have a clear path to sustainable growth.

Critical Questions to Address
  • How will you measure success in the first year? (Answer: Focus on learning KPIs from the MVP).
  • What are the top 3 risks? (Answer: User adoption, tech hurdles, competition for attention - and your mitigations).
Startup Framing

Detail the **Go-to-Market (GTM) Strategy** (Beachhead -> Expansion) and the **Competitive Moat** (Network Effects, Data, Trust, Holistic Approach).

Step 6: The Ask (The "Recommendation")

Pitcher's Action

Make a clear, decisive, and confident recommendation. Don't just present options. State your "One-Sentence Ask" for the MVP phase and initial budget.

Critical Questions to Address
  • What if your assumptions are wrong? (Answer: That's why we're doing an MVP - to test and learn).
  • Do we have the right team/resources? (Answer: We have the vision; the ask includes resources for technical execution).
Startup Framing

This is the **Call to Action**. In a startup pitch, this is where you ask for the investment. Here, you are asking for the board's investment of trust, approval, and initial resources.

Phase 3: Q&A and Follow-Up

The Conversation & The Close

The pitch doesn't end when you stop talking. Secure your success through a masterful Q&A and professional follow-up.

1. Welcome Questions

Frame the Q&A as a discussion, not a defense. Use phrases like, "That's an excellent question..."

2. Use the "Bridge" Technique

Answer tough questions briefly, then "bridge" back to a core message of your pitch.

3. Send a Summary Email

Within 24 hours, send a concise email reiterating the "Ask" and summarizing key decisions and action items.

4. Follow Through Immediately

Begin work on agreed-upon actions to demonstrate momentum and build confidence.
